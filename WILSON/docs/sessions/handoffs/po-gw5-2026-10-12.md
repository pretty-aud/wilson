## 3. Done and verified — the findings

The brief's rule: every finding closed with a test and a planted fault, or named here with why it stays. One finding from the attack; its two review rounds found five more.

### Finding 1 (High, FIXED) — one peer could exhaust a door's connection pool (pre-auth DoS)
`gateway/src/rules/limits.mjs`, `gateway/src/doors/gates.mjs` (`58d9e074`). The per-peer limits bounded the RATE a peer opened connections (60/min outside, 120/min inside) and the global `Pools` reserve protected the inside door from the outside door, but nothing bounded how many connections ONE peer HELD OPEN. Measured against the real gate logic: one outside address fills the whole outside pool (300), one inside LAN host fills the whole global pool (400), starving the office and every other viewer — from a single address, before any ticket. This is exactly what §10 row 10 and F11 say cannot happen. Fix: a per-peer concurrent-connection cap on both doors (`connectionsPerPeer`), acquired after the rate checks, released with the pool on socket close. Verified: `policy.test.mjs` (one peer capped, a second peer + the office keep room); three planted faults caught; live at the real listener on loopback (16/30 from one peer, control 30/30, a freed slot reopens); `localProof.mjs` unbroken.

### Review round 1 (one security reviewer, model opus, read-only, loopback only) — `6e979796`
- **A (Medium, FIXED):** the inside cap (100) equalled the guaranteed inside reserve (`connections × insideReserve` = 100), so one inside host still ate the whole reserve when the outside door was full. Round 1 lowered the inside default to 32; round 2 found that hardcode insufficient (see below) and the two are one fix now.
- **B (Medium, FIXED):** `outsideSource` trusted `CF-Connecting-IP` first and unconditionally whenever the peer was the declared proxy. Behind a NAS reverse proxy (`behind_local_proxy`), Tailscale or ngrok — none of which set or strip it — a remote viewer forging it poisoned the viewing's audit `source_address` / `shared_url` (§6/G6/D7). Fix: honour `CF-Connecting-IP` only when the admin declared the proxy as Cloudflare; every other proxy uses the `X-Forwarded-For` last hop it appends. Test in `addresses.test.mjs`, planted fault caught. Round 2 confirmed HELD, and that `declaredProxy.name` is admin-config only (no attacker path to `'cloudflare'`).
- **D (Low, FIXED):** `resolveLimits` did not bound `connectionsPerPeer`, so a config value re-disabled the cap. Folded into A's fix (clamp to a third of the door's share).
- **E (Low, FIXED):** `serveClip`'s serve phase ran unguarded after the stream slot, pool slot and file handle were acquired; a throw there reached the handler's 500 WITHOUT releasing them, leaking one per-person stream slot, one global stream slot and one file handle each time. Fix: wrap the serve phase; a throw before the stream is handed to the pipeline releases all three. Test in `doorsOutside.test.mjs` (ten throws, no leak, the door still serves); planted fault caught. Round 2 hardened the releases to be once-guarded (see below).
- **C (Low, CARRIED — a residual, not fixed):** see below.

### Review round 2 (one security reviewer, model opus, read-only, loopback only) — `3718f4f7`
- **A/D (Medium/Low, FIXED as one):** round 1 hardcoded the inside cap at 32, which subdivides the reserve only at the default pool. A smaller `connections` (legal, floor 16) or a lower `insideReserve` made the cap ≥ the reserve again, re-opening finding A; and round 1's clamp clamped only to the whole door share ("no cap"). `resolveLimits` now caps `connectionsPerPeer` at a third of the door's RESOLVED guaranteed share for every config (inside = the reserve; outside = the hard max), floored at `DOOR_FLOOR`; the default 32/100 is kept at the default pool and auto-tightens for a smaller one; an explicit value above a third of the share is clamped and reported. `policy.test.mjs` proves `connections:128` → inside cap 10 and the office keeps 22; two planted faults caught.
- **E (Low, HARDENED):** the round-1 guard works (no leak), but its comment wrongly credited `Pools.closeStream`'s floor-at-zero for idempotency — it is NOT idempotent (a second close decrements a different stream's slot). The stream-slot release is now once-guarded at the call site; the comment is corrected. The finding-E test and its planted fault still stand.
- **B, E held** for the leak/forgery they fix (round 2 attacked and confirmed).
- **C (Low, CARRIED):** the inside door keys its per-peer limits by full address (deliberate: a LAN's /64 is every office computer, so folding it would collectively cap the whole office's rate AND concurrency, breaking a real office), while the outside door folds IPv6 to /64 (R2-4). So one inside IPv6 host binding several addresses in its /64 (ordinary SLAAC/privacy addressing) gets a fresh per-address budget for each and can hold the whole inside capacity, starving other office hosts in its /64 and the outside door. **Not fixed, with a demonstration:** the attacker's addresses and the office's hosts share the one office /64 (measured), so the reviewer's proposed per-/64 concurrency ceiling bounds them TOGETHER — it cannot separate one host from its office — and would cap a real single-/64 office collectively; there is no L3 way to tell one-host-many-addresses from many-hosts-one-/64. It is the malicious-LAN-insider residual the design concedes (§10 row 22; row 10 is the matching flood row); the inside reserve still guarantees the office is never starved by the OUTSIDE door. **The design was corrected** (the GW5 review-history section; §5 and §10 row 10 now say the guarantee holds for one IPv4 host, not an IPv6 host binding several /64 addresses). Round 1's commit message had claimed this softening was already written — it was not; round 2 caught that, and it is written now.

## 5. Traps hit

- **Bash heredocs halve `\\` and run escaped backticks / break on apostrophes** (the documented trap): the fuzzers and the long design-doc section were written with the Write tool and appended with a plain `cat >>` redirect; imports used absolute `file://` URLs.
- **openssl's MinGW (Git for Windows) s_client will not OFFER TLS 1.0/1.1 at its own security level**, so it tests the client, not the server. Prove the server's floor with Node's `tls.connect` pinned to `TLSv1_1_method` + `ciphers: 'ALL:@SECLEVEL=0'` — the server returns a `TLSV1_ALERT_PROTOCOL_VERSION`.
- **A per-minute `FixedWindow` resets its budget each window**, so connections a peer opens at its rate accumulate across windows up to the global pool — the core of finding 1 (the rate cap is not a concurrency cap).
- **`Pools.closeStream`/`closeConnection` are not idempotent** (a double close decrements a different slot; floor-at-zero only hides it for the last open one) — release through a call-site once-guard, do not rely on the pool (finding E, round 2).
- **To isolate the concurrency cap from the rate limiter in a unit experiment**, raise `newConnectionsPerMinutePerPeer`/`handshakesPerSecondPerPeer` out of the way (they have no CEILING) and advance the fake clock, or all calls at one instant are throttled by the per-second handshake window.
- **A reviewer subagent's experiments are this session's binds**: both rounds were told loopback only and `127.0.0.1` explicitly (never 0.0.0.0, which includes the LAN); both kept to it. Their reports came back as the subagent's final message (the harness may refuse a report file), saved by the session.
