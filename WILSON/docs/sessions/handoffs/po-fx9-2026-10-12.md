## 3. Done and verified

1. **The race, measured before anything was changed** (a scratch harness:
   the old sequence 20 and 40 times in one process, every `gateway-sync`
   call timed through a wrapped fake-cloud `fetch`). The sync that wins is
   not the 10 s heartbeat. It is the runtime's own follow-up: a sync answer
   with `check_reach_now` starts a reach check, a stat in a fresh worker
   thread (`gateway/src/cloud/reach.mjs`, capped at 2 s), and asks for
   one more sync when it lands (`runtime.mjs` `#onSync`,
   `this.#checkReach().then(() => this.sync?.requestNow())`).
   - **Idle, 20 runs:** the test sets the fresh nonce about 8 ms after the
     check_reach_now sync, the door answers 0–1 ms later, and the follow-up
     lands about 17 ms after the sync, carrying the fresh nonce. So the
     margin was about 8 ms. 0 of 20 failed.
   - **Beside the full suite, 40 runs:** **1 failed**, with the follow-up
     inside the window, 27 ms after the previous sync, carrying the fresh
     nonce, CI's exact failure. The follow-up landed 7–29 ms after the sync
     across the 40 runs: 15 times before the fresh nonce, 24 after the
     answer, 1 in between.
   - **The heartbeat cannot reach today's window:** it fires 10 s after the
     last sync, and each test has its own gateway and a 5 s time-out.
2. **The fix** (`54472510`, test only). `holdSyncs(W)` waits for the sync in
   flight to land, then stops the gateway's sync loop. The last in-flight
   check and the stop are one synchronous step. While held:
   - no heartbeat and no on-demand sync can answer (the check_reach_now
     follow-up, an unknown kid, a catalogue check);
   - one asked for meanwhile is dropped, not deferred;
   - `release()` restarts the loop.

   `syncCalls(W)` counts the fake cloud's sync calls. The test then:
   - holds the syncs;
   - sets the fresh nonce and probes;
   - reads the window whole in one `toEqual`: the probe's `isThisGateway`
     (false), `proof_checked` (true), the body (exactly `{ ok: true }`, which
     was a subset match before), the syncs in the window (0), and the nonce
     the runtime holds (`gw.remote.reachNonce`, the old one);
   - probes the old nonce during the hold (must still prove it, so the false
     is the nonce, not a door that stopped proving);
   - releases, syncs, and proves the fresh nonce, as before.

   A failure now says which of two things happened: a sync came early (the
   race) or the door proved a nonce no sync gave it (the product fault the
   assertion exists for).

## 5. Traps hit

1. **The race's culprit was not the one named.** OUTSTANDING and the hand-offs
   inferred the periodic sync. Measured, it is `check_reach_now`'s follow-up
   sync, about 17 ms after the answer. The 10 s heartbeat is 10 s after the
   LAST sync, so it cannot land inside a test with the default 5 s time-out.
   The hold covers both.
2. **A loop is weak evidence for a 1 ms race on a big computer.** The cut's
   test passed 219 runs out of 219 here under load. Stretch the window
   deterministically (variants A/B/OLD/E1) to prove the hold, and keep a
   control beside any loop.
3. **Planting a line with `&&` through `bash` and `node -e`** wrote `\&\&`:
   a syntax error that "fails" for the wrong reason. Plant through a script
   file (`plant.mjs`) and run `node --check` on the mutated file before you
   read a failure as caught.
4. **A test's `console.log` never reached the output** of a redirected
   `npx vitest run` here. Write a scratch measurement to a file
   (`fs.appendFileSync`).
