### What the two rounds changed, in one paragraph each

**Round 1's three Highs.** A tunnel or proxy aimed at the inside door would have served remote viewers as office peers, with the switch off, unlogged and without the overlay; the inside door now binds on LAN interfaces only, closes loopback and own-host peers before TLS, refuses proxy headers, and the raw-TCP-relay residual is named and watched for. The gateway's real boundary was "a file under a location root", so a stolen ticket-signing key would have read the whole share within the allow-list; the gateway now confirms every clip with the cloud (the catalogue check) and the key has a threat-model row. The audit function took the viewer, the clip and the workspace from the gateway's word; it now derives every attribution itself and refuses a viewing for a viewer and clip that were never minted (every mint is recorded).

**Round 2's two Highs.** Both corrections above assumed the gateway sees real addresses, which a container on Docker's bridge network does not: it would have registered a bridge address no browser can reach, minted a certificate with no name for the NAS's address, and seen every peer as the bridge's gateway. The container now runs on the host's network, and a gateway whose only network is a bridge keeps its inside door closed and says what to change. The rest of round 2 tightened the renewal's bindings (a four-hour stream, a leaked URL's real life said), kept mints thirty days and flagged aged-out audit rows rather than dropping them, reversed round 1's fail-closed limiter on the ticket mint (a database hiccup must not end every playback), dropped the NAS vendors' relays as unsupported, withdrew a published address the moment the company's public address changes, gave the Windows updater its own least-privilege service with a second verification before the swap, replaced the local service account with a Windows-managed one, required the enrolling admin to confirm the printed certificate fingerprint before the root is offered for download, and narrowed the private root's permitted addresses to a /24.

## 5. Traps hit

1. **The app's untracked `.claude/launch.json` at the worktree root blocks `git checkout` of the integration branch** (which tracks that file). Move it to the scratchpad before the checkout; do not delete it. The tracked copy then arrives with the branch.
2. **`server.closeAllConnections()` does not close a connection that has not finished its TLS handshake**, and `server.close()` then waits for Node's 120 s `handshakeTimeout` (measured: 120,002 ms). A listener that must disappear on demand tracks every socket on the `'connection'` event (TCP level, before TLS) and destroys them itself, with a short `handshakeTimeout` as the second bound. This is now §9 step 4 of the design.
3. **The administrative share `\\localhost\C$` is readable from this computer** (BC2's hand-off said no SMB share was reachable here; the admin share answers, at least over loopback), so a UNC-path spike needs no WebDAV redirector. A location refuses `localhost` and `C$` by design; the spike was about the redirector's cost, which it measured at about a millisecond per 64 KB read.
4. **The Bash tool persists any output over roughly 30 KB to a file instead of showing it.** Read long documents in slices (`sed -n 'a,bp'`); the TPN design review, BC3's hand-off and handbook §17 each overflowed.
5. **A design reviewer takes about twenty minutes and 200,000 tokens per round at Opus.** Both rounds together were the session's largest cost; each was worth it (two Highs each).
6. **Docker's default bridge network hides the host's addresses and every client's address from a container.** Any design that classifies by peer address or puts the host's address in a certificate must require host networking for the container, and should refuse to open its door on a bridge rather than misbehave.
7. **Web facts the design leans on carry caveats the text keeps**: Chrome's local-network permission (the prompt since Chrome 142; sources disagree on the exact rollout date); Supabase's JWKS path (`/auth/v1/.well-known/jwks.json`, with `/auth/v1/jwks` also cited); name constraints on user-installed roots (enforced on Windows, macOS and Firefox; Chrome on Linux historically not). GW1 and GW3 verify each on the day.

## 6. Waiting on Audrey

1. **D1. The gateway never sees the sign-in token.** The cloud verifies the sign-in and mints a two-minute, one-clip ticket; the gateway checks the cloud's signature on every read. (a) **Yes, tickets (the pick: a `<video>` cannot send a header, and a rogue gateway must not become a token harvester)**; (b) send the sign-in token to the gateway in the URL instead (G4's literal words).
2. **D2. Two doors.** The office door on 8443 and the outside door on 8444; the company forwards only 8444; a public address reaching 8443 is closed before TLS. (a) **Yes (the pick)**; (b) one door with the switch deciding per request (round 1 showed why not).
3. **D3. The office certificate.** A private root (ten years, constrained to the gateway's names and the /24 around its addresses) installed once per office computer, with a 397-day leaf the gateway renews itself. (a) **Yes (the pick)**; (b) a single self-signed certificate re-installed on every computer every two years (no root key on the box, more admin work).
4. **D4. The outside certificate.** The free one from Let's Encrypt built in (the company forwards port 443; GW4 builds it), with the company's own certificate as the alternative. (a) **Yes, built in (the pick: a remote reviewer's own laptop is the one computer nobody can install a root on)**; (b) the company always brings its own certificate.
5. **D5. The ticket's life.** Two minutes, renewed by the player at 75 s, a stream at most four hours; a revoked person stops within about three minutes. (a) **Yes (the pick)**; (b) one minute (twice the cloud calls per playing clip).

7. **D7. One audit row per viewing** (a viewing ends after 60 s without a request, or at five minutes), not one per Range request. (a) **Yes (the pick)**; (b) one row per five-minute segment regardless.
